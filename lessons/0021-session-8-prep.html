Session thesis

Session thesis: salvage is not repair. Some things come back only in the condition you found them, and the honest work is carrying them out anyway — with a true account of what is lost.

Ot asks for damaged books and means it; the Treatise is beyond saving and he wants a plain account of it rather than an apology. Zuma's ink then asks the same question from the other end: what can pass between people when the working thing is imperfect? His ritual fails at what he designed it to do and succeeds at something better — words go between them, magic does not. Thematic guide: Word and Way (study unused is incomplete) and curiosity's shadow (a scholar's unfinished thesis is why this building was abandoned). Most needed by: Cy'An and Solanum; Lithrei is the one who can see "abandoned" as a career rather than a failure.

Return to Ot — the debrief

Award 30 XP per book returned, maximum 120 — four is the ceiling and four is what they have. Plus 80 XP if they showed the spellskeins out (they are in B8, not B6 — see the room key), and 50 gp for the create undead notes. Do not tell them the cap in advance; let the fourth book land as the good news and the Treatise as the bad.

Play Ot at his desk, books arranged around him, the lens still on the map. He is fussy about the list and genuinely moved by damage — that combination is the whole character.

  • Four books, one of them a chest. Mata Digemari's notes arriving as furniture is a good joke; let him be delighted by it and then immediately practical about where the chest goes.
  • Damaged books. He means what he said: bring them back regardless of condition. He turns a water-swollen page, says something dry about restoration, and moves on. No scolding, no disappointment.
  • The destroyed Treatise. This is the one that lands. He asks what happened to it, listens, and does not blame anyone. Then: "Then it's gone. Thank you for telling me plainly." Pause there — the thesis of the night is in that line.
  • "Anything else?" He means it, and he is delighted by oddities. The folio gets set aside with visible interest; the create undead notes get 50 gp and the faintest look of disgust. Academic supplies are uninteresting to him and he says so.
  • The building. Taraan's read of the stump is the report he actually wants. If they say it is salvageable, he is quietly pleased and says he may send someone. He does not commission a second trip tonight, and he still does not know why it was abandoned.
  • Gratitude, not a quest. The beat is that he thanks them as colleagues, not as errand-runners. He will ask them again — that is the payoff. If Cy'An's quiet repair in the Archives has already happened, letting Ot notice it here (or one floor away) is the natural close on that beat.
  • The hand-off. Ot is the one who mentions Zuma: the Emerald Bough teacher has been asking after volunteers. That is the bridge into the back half of the night, and it comes from Ot rather than out of nowhere.

Testing the effects — play this, don't summarize it

He wants magic shared. It is not. Three tests, in order, each one deflating him a little further while he scribbles happily:

  1. Cast your best spell. It comes out at its normal level. Nothing is amplified.
  2. Cast a spell you have seen a companion cast but cannot cast yourself. Nobody can. He scribbles harder, anxious now.
  3. Try to speak to each other without sound. This works. The mouths move, the words arrive, and the table discovers a private channel in a room with a half-orc watching.

The result is not what he promised and is better than what he promised, and he does not quite notice the difference. He gets good notes either way, thanks them, and sends them out so he can get back to work.

Award and treasure. 30 XP for taking part; 80 XP if the ritual succeeds at all (not just crit). He gives a cheap-looking costume hat to whoever was most interested in the ritual — a genuine hat of the magi, no stronger than an ordinary one, which he insists protects against "unwanted mental influences." Whoever takes it should put it on for the rest of the scene.

GM notes

  • Do not make the failure a betrayal. Zuma did not lie and he is not embarrassed. He is a real scholar testing a real formula and he got a real result.
  • Do not let the whispers be a plot device. Tonight it is a toy: five mouths that can gossip across a room. If they use it in front of faculty, Zuma is delighted and Ot is not.
  • Lithrei's angle. Zuma is his designated "light" faculty contact — the one who tells him the school is hiding something. One line tonight is enough, said by accident while Zuma talks about defenses. Do not let Zuma become his mystery.
  • Cy'An's angle. Not a beat, just room: a goblin cook who builds things meeting a man who builds the wrong thing on purpose and does not consider it failure. Let Cy'An like him.
  • The hat is a joke that is also a real item. Note who takes it. It will matter less than they think and more than Zuma thinks.
