Session thesis

Session thesis: salvage is not repair. Some things come back only in the condition you found them, and the honest work is carrying them out anyway — with a true account of what is lost.

Ot asks for damaged books and means it; one of the five cannot be recovered at all, and the room where it died is quiet. The spellskeins cannot be fixed, only let out. And Cy'An — whose whole wound is usefulness — spends the night proving that a hinge and a true inventory are worth more than a finished job. Thematic guide: Word and Way (study unused is incomplete) and curiosity's shadow (a scholar's unfinished thesis is why this building was abandoned). Most needed by: Cy'An and Solanum; Lithrei is the one who can read the ruin as a career rather than a failure.

GM only — still just a library tonight

Return to Ot — the debrief

Award 30 XP per book returned, maximum 120 — four books is the ceiling. Plus 80 XP for freeing the spellskeins, and 50 gp if they hand over the create undead notes. Do not tell them the cap in advance.

Play Ot at his desk, books arranged around him, the lens still on the map. He is fussy about the list and genuinely moved by damage — that combination is the whole character.

  • Damaged books. He means what he said: return them regardless of condition. He turns a water-swollen page, says something dry about restoration, and moves on. No scolding, no disappointment.
  • The destroyed Treatise. This is the one that lands. He asks what happened to it, listens, and does not blame anyone. Then: "Then it's gone. Thank you for telling me plainly." Pause there — the thesis of the night is in that line.
  • "Anything else?" He means it, and he is delighted by oddities. The folio gets set aside with visible interest; the create undead notes get 50 gp and the faintest look of disgust. Academic supplies are uninteresting to him and he says so.
  • The building. If they report that it's salvageable, he is quietly pleased and says he may send someone. He does not commission a second trip tonight. He does not know why it was abandoned, and says so again.
  • Gratitude, not a quest. The beat is that he thanks them as colleagues, not as errand-runners. He will ask them again — that is the payoff, and it is why the hinge matters (below).

Table risks and GM moves

  • Search sprawl is the real time sink. Resolve a room's search in one roll and move to the next door. A searched room is searched; the five books have fixed locations.
  • Bed trap twice is a grudge. 2d6 to everyone at level 2 is a real bite, and it lands on the party's weakest saves. Spring it once. Let Taraan's read prevent or soften it.
  • One silverfish fight, not two. B3 and B5 are the same encounter split across two rooms. If they've already cleared one, the other is empty and quiet.
  • The spellskeins are not a fight. If someone attacks them out of reflex, they defend and flee — they do not become a combat encounter and they do not cost XP for being spared. The 80 XP is for the exit, not the kill.
  • Do not let the wasp nest become a puzzle. There is no way to save the scroll. If a player insists, Ot's answer is the same: it's gone. That is the point, and it is a kindness not to dangle false hope.
  • Six players, five beats, one debrief. Do not run them as a queue in Ot's office. Stagger them: Taraan in B4, Solanum in B6, Lithrei in B7, Cy'An at a door on the way out, Yggostrus in the corridor. Only Cy'An's needs Ot in the room.
  • Level does not change tonight. Level 3 arrives at First Masking (S14). Track the XP anyway — the book awards it and the table has been counting.
  • Tone: the front half was a fight. This half is quieter and stranger — an empty building with one good chair in it, and a nest made of somebody's book. Let it be melancholy rather than spooky.
