Academia downtime

How Magaambya branch levels work, what Study and Cram do while you’re a student, and how Practical Research advances you once you teach.

What academia downtime is

Magaambya branch levels advance through semester-scale downtime checks, not XP. While you’re a student you Study (or Cram to catch up); once you teach, you advance through Practical Research in the field. The payoff is a parallel track of lore, skill feats, and Magaambyan Attendant archetype feats tied to each branch.

These rules sit on top of normal Pathfinder 2e downtime. When the story gives you free days, you can still Earn Income, Learn a Spell, and so on — and this table uses Campus Crafting (on the Rules tab) instead of published Craft for party gear. Academia checks answer a different question (branch level, not pocket money).

Sources: Archives of Nethys — Life in the Academy, Kindled Magic pp. 61–63.

Two level tracks

  • Each PC picks a primary branch and a secondary branch (both start at level 0).
  • Primary branch level cannot exceed character level.
  • Secondary branch level cannot exceed half character level (minimum 0).
  • Every branch level grants a benefit (lore, skill feats, Magaambyan Attendant dedication at branch 3, and so on) — see Branch benefits.
  • Each academia downtime activity represents roughly a semester (a few months). Adventures happen during or between semesters as the story requires.

The star rule (banking progress)

If a check would raise a branch past its cap, mark a ★ next to that branch level. When you next gain a character level, bump the branch to the new cap and erase the star. Good rolls are never wasted.

Three downtime activities

Study

Requirements: initiate, attendant, or conversant.

Pick primary or secondary branch. Roll one of that branch’s associated skills against the standard DC for the branch’s current level.

ResultEffect
Critical success+2 branch levels
Success+1 branch level
FailureNo change
Critical failureSkip the next Study opportunity (remedial work)

AoN — Study

Cram

Requirements: initiate, attendant, or conversant; primary or secondary branch is 2+ levels below its cap.

Perform Study twice (each branch must qualify). Until you take Study again normally, each adventuring day starts with a DC 8 flat check or you are fatigued that day.

Catch-up, not default: Cram is for when a branch has fallen behind its cap. The fatigue cost bites during busy adventuring stretches — use it when you need the levels more than the rest.

AoN — Cram

Practical Research

Requirements: conversant or lore-speaker.

Field discovery — when the opportunity arises, attempt an appropriate check for the situation. Results usually mirror Study; some opportunities grant unique options (new spells, access to locations, and similar).

After graduation: Lore-speakers teach, but teaching alone does not advance branch levels. Field discovery — adding scholarship, recovering lost knowledge — is how teachers progress.

Additional Lore (1st)

You learn more of the Magaambya’s deep well of lore associated with your branch. You gain the Additional Lore feat in a Lore skill associated with the branch.

Steeped in History (2nd)

You learn about the history of the branch and the Magaambya in general. You gain a +1 circumstance bonus to Recall Knowledge about the Magaambya, the tenets or history of your branch, and members of your branch. At 11th level, this increases to a +2 circumstance bonus.

Dedicated Attendant (3rd)

If you have completed your Perquisite, you have proven your commitment enough that your branch promotes and supports you as an affiliated attendant. The first time you reach this level in a branch, you gain the Magaambyan Attendant Dedication feat as a bonus feat (even if you haven’t taken enough feats with a previous archetype to take another dedication feat).

Skill Feat (4th)

You uncover hidden techniques that grant extra benefits from your branch’s associated skills. You gain a skill feat that requires the trained rank in one of the branch’s associated skills. At 9th level, you gain an additional skill feat that requires the expert or trained rank in one of the branch’s associated skills; at 15th level, you gain an additional skill feat that requires master or lower rank in one of the branch’s associated skills; and at 19th level, you gain an additional skill feat that requires legendary or lower rank in one of the branch’s associated skills. For any of these skill feats, you can select a variable skill feat, such as Automatic Knowledge or Assurance, as long as you do so for an associated skill.

Branch Influence (5th)

You gain greater influence, making it easier to collaborate, hoodwink, or browbeat members of that branch. You gain a +1 circumstance bonus on Deception, Diplomacy, Intimidation, and Performance checks to interact with members of the branch. At 13th level, this increases to a +2 circumstance bonus, and at 17th level, it increases again to a +3 circumstance bonus.

Skill Increase (6th)

At 6th level in a branch, and every 4 levels thereafter, your studies lead you to deeper understanding of your branch’s associated skills, causing you to gain an additional skill increase that you can use to raise one of the branch’s associated skills, up to a maximum of expert. At 10th branch level or higher, you can use these skill increases to become a master in a skill in which you’re already an expert. At 18th level, you can use this skill increase to become legendary in a skill in which you’re already a master. For Uzunjati, who have Lore as an associated skill, you gain the Additional Lore feat when you choose to improve Lore, instead of gaining a single skill increase to a Lore.

Branch Feat (7th)

You become so immersed in your branch’s secrets that you gain access to special training only offered to other members of your branch. You gain the 6th-level Magaambyan attendant archetype feat requiring affiliation with your branch (such as Uzunjati Storytelling for the Uzunjati). You must still meet any skill prerequisites the feat has. If you already have that feat, you gain an additional 6th-level class feat (even if your branch’s feat was not a class feat). At 12th level, you also gain the 10th-level Magaambyan attendant archetype feat requiring affiliation with the branch (such as Tempest-Sun Shielding for the Tempest-Sun Mages). If you already have that feat, you gain an additional 10th-level class feat (even if your branch’s feat was not a class feat).

General Feat (8th)

Your understanding of your branch’s traditions improves your general capabilities. You gain the general feat associated with the branch (see the branch skills table above). If you already have that general feat, you gain a 1st-level general feat of your choice instead.

Make Your Own Luck (16th)

You’re so accomplished with your branch’s associated skills that you make your own luck. Once per day, you can reroll a skill check with one of those skills and take the new result. This is a fortune effect.

Magical Paragon (20th)

You embody the branch so completely that you gain a unique special ability depending on your branch. Many of these abilities build upon Magaambyan attendant archetype feats you have gained.

  • Cascade Bearers: Nothing is beyond your imagination. Once per day, you can cast any common 7th-level spell from the arcane, divine, occult, or primal spell lists without expending a spell slot. When you do, you use your spellcasting proficiency for your halcyon spells.
  • Emerald Boughs: You can stretch your hideaway well beyond the original intended usage, creating a special extradimensional abode in which to rest with your allies, host visiting diplomats, and more. At any time, you can spend 1 minute to enter and expand your hideaway into a magnificent mansion; it remains in this form until you leave, at which point it expels all remaining guests and becomes a normal hideaway as Emerald Boughs Hideaway until you spend another minute to enter and expand your hideaway again.
  • Rain-Scribes: You can adapt to almost any situation. You can survive in severe and extreme cold and heat without taking damage. You move at full Speed through difficult terrain and greater difficult terrain, and take no damage from hazardous terrain. When using Rain-Scribe Mobility, you clear the effects of all three types of terrain from the spaces you enter for 1 round.
  • Tempest-Sun Mages: You have the courage and magic to protect all your allies from harm. When you use Tempest-Sun Shielding, you can choose to reduce the triggering damage to you and all allies within 30 feet by four times the spell level, rather than reducing the triggering damage to you or a single ally by four times the spell level.
  • Uzunjati: You know a surprising number of stories, and you weave them all together into a coherent narrative. When you use Uzunjati Recollection, you Recall Knowledge three times instead of once, each with a +2 circumstance bonus. You also gain a +2 circumstance bonus to your skill check for Uzunjati Storytelling from the captivating combined narrative.

Other downtime (still available)

Academia activities are story-scheduled — they come up at set points in the campaign. Standard PF2e downtime is optional filler whenever you have free time: Earn Income, Campus Crafting (Rules tab; replaces published Craft for party gear), Learn a Spell, Subsist, Retrain, buying and selling gear, long-term rest, and cost of living.

SoT vs default: Standard downtime answers “what do I do with spare days?” Academia downtime answers “how does my branch level rise?” Both matter.

Pathbuilder — branch feats & archetypes

Branch benefits often grant bonus feats (and eventually Magaambyan Attendant archetype feats) that do not use your normal level feat slots. Pathbuilder can track those; you just have to add them as extras rather than picking them at a level-up.

1. Add a bonus feat

  1. Open the menu (☰) and choose Custom Feat Choices (sometimes near Custom Pack / optional rules).
  2. Add a new feat of the right type — skill feat, general feat, class feat, or archetype feat — and pick the published option you just earned from a branch level.
  3. That feat is recorded on your sheet without spending a class / skill / general feat from character level.

Use this for Additional Lore, branch skill feats, the branch’s general feat at level 8, Magaambyan Attendant (branch) feats at 7 and 12, and any “you already had that feat, take a class feat instead” replacements.

2. Magaambyan Attendant dedication (branch 3)

Pathbuilder cannot ship “Magaambya” product-identity names. Search for Collegiate Attendant Dedication instead — same feat, licensing rename — then choose your branch affiliation when prompted.

  1. Add it via Custom Feat Choices as an archetype / dedication feat (bonus feat from Dedicated Attendant).
  2. If Pathbuilder warns that you haven’t finished another archetype yet, that’s fine for this campaign: Dedicated Attendant grants the dedication even if you haven’t taken two feats from a prior archetype.

3. Skill increases from branch levels

Branch skill increases (levels 6, 10, 14, 18) aren’t feats. Raise the skill on your Skills tab to the new rank, and note in a comment or custom buff that the increase came from your branch so you don’t lose track at the next level-up.

If a menu label doesn’t match exactly, look under the ☰ menu for “Custom Feat” / “Feat Choices” — Pathbuilder’s wording varies slightly by platform and version.
