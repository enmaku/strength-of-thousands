Session thesis

Session thesis: a library that was abandoned because a project got hard can still hold everything worth saving — and someone has to go in and do the unglamorous work of finding it. Ot asks for five specific books and is happy with damaged ones; the scrits are destroying the same collection that made them excellent readers. The cohort has to choose between the tidy list and the messy salvage, and Ot’s gratitude goes to whoever brings back what nobody asked for.

Who needs it most: Solanum (failed her Study; the errand is where an unfinished semester stops being a verdict), Lithrei (the investigator finally gets a whole building to read), Cy’An (a fixer confronted with a job that cannot be finished in one night — which Session 7 demonstrated literally). Thematic guide: Word and Way — study unused is incomplete; Book 1’s “institution has blind spots” (nobody on campus remembers why the conversion stopped).

The ask — Ot’s office

Played in Session 7 This scene is done. Keep it here as the record of what Ot asked for; do not replay it. Session 8 opens inside the stump (or on the walk back to it), not in Ot's office.

Top floor of the Heron Archives, same building as Koride. Read or paraphrase; the furrowed brow and the lens over the map are the whole scene. Keep it under five minutes of table time.

“I have a favor to ask of you. Some research materials I need are missing from the usual reference libraries. I’ve had initiates look for them with no success, and I’ve spoken with the other professors to see if perhaps what I need has landed in their more private collections. Again, no success. I now know where they aren’t, which tells me where they must be.

This building here hasn’t seen use in…” — a shrug — “It was carved from an enormous stump as a dormitory but was being converted into a library before the project was abandoned. Please see if these five books are there. Return them to me regardless of their condition. I would appreciate anything else you feel would be relevant or worth salvaging.”

  • He does not know why the conversion was abandoned. Nobody still on campus does. If asked, he says exactly that, and it is true.
  • He does not send initiates into the tunnels, does not mention gremlins, and does not connect the library to the ceremony. He has been told odd pest reports and has filed them.
  • If they mention the centipedes, he is interested but not alarmed — “then be careful in there” — and does not assign Koride’s survey.
  • Books are 30 XP each, max 120 (one is unrecoverable). Do not tell them the cap.

The tree-stump library

Moderate 2 printed · six level-2 PCs A stump wrapped in vines, lightning-scarred on top, dusty windows. The vegetation by the door has been hacked away — pulled apart, not cut — with ink stains on some vines. A garland of silver bells lies discarded on the step, torn from the door.

The bells are the tell: scrits believe only silver bells are dangerous and take a −2 circumstance penalty to attack anyone openly carrying them. If the party picks them up and carries them visibly, B1 gets much easier and the session gets much more fun. The garland is worth 10 gp and is also a fey-abeyance-adjacent prop — the cohort already made cold-iron bells for the pugwampi in Session 3, so someone may connect the dots. Let them.

Doors and light

Dim light throughout; the interior is unlit. Every interior door is lightweight wood with the latches removed — a push, no roll. Eight rooms off the main hall (B1–B8). Shelves are 8 ft, ceilings 10 ft, and toppling a bad shelf is DC 13 Athletics.

Table risks and GM moves

  • Six players vs. four scrits: the printed fight is small. They are a hazard, not a boss — grease, shelves, ink poison, and flight. If they die in one round, that is correct. If the party is carrying silver bells, say the −2 out loud so the table sees the trick pay off.
  • Nocturnal swarm bookkeeping: silverfish are one moderate fight, whether it starts in B3 or B5. Do not run them twice.
  • Search sprawl: the building invites a shelf-by-shelf crawl. Resolve searching a room in one roll and move to the next door. The five books have fixed locations — if a room has been searched, it is searched.
  • Bed trap: 2d6 to everyone in B4 is a real bite at level 2, especially with Solanum at 0 branch and the party used to being healed. Let Taraan’s Architecture suggestion reduce or bypass it; do not spring it twice.
  • Anya catching up: if she is back, she needs a branch, an implement, and a Study result before or after the errand — not a replay of Session 6. Two sentences of recap, then let her pick a branch off the branch handout. Make the Study roll DC 14 with whatever she picks. Do not make it a public reckoning. If she is not back, say so once and continue.
  • The absent-player question: the table raised a possible hiatus for Julia last session. Do not open that discussion mid-errand; if it comes up, keep it to “we’ll sort it after tonight.”
  • Reading the wrong book: if a player asks about the Bloodsalt folio or the create undead notes, answer with the object, not the plot. The map matters in Book 3; tonight it is just old cartography.
  • Tone: this is the first dungeon in a long while that is genuinely just a dungeon. Let it be a little spooky and a little silly. Not every abandoned room needs a secret.
